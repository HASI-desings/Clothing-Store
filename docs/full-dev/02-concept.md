# Creative Concept: "Cut Light" · Tailor's Pattern × Jali Screen

_Status: **draft** (2026-11-20). Awaiting Phase 2 approval._

## Objects
- **A (identity object): the tailor's paper pattern.** The flat blueprint of a garment: cut paper pieces marked with notches, grainlines, fold lines and "CUT 2". It is literally where clothing is *constructed* before it is worn. It is the truest object behind "style is constructed".
- **B (unexpected object): the jali.** A pierced marble or sandstone lattice screen from Mughal architecture (Lahore Fort, Jahangir's Tomb, Shalimar). It comes from stonemasonry and light control, not from fashion.
- **Candidates considered:**
  - A: measuring tape (too literal and "tailor shop"); a seam (too small to build a system on); a mannequin (fashion cliché).
  - B: paper kite / Basant (playful, but fights "sophisticated"); a seismograph (cold); bookbinding signatures (good, kept as an alternative); a rammed-earth wall (strata, too rough); a loom (too close to the industry); a chessboard (generic "strategy" metaphor); a sundial (light, but no construction); a brick kiln (heavy, low fashion).
  - Shortlisted pairs: Pattern × **Jali** (winner) · Pattern × Kite · Garment × Bookbinding.

## Five forced connections
1. **Both are flat things that create space.** A pattern turns paper into a 3D garment; a jali turns a flat slab into a room full of shadow.
2. **Both are defined by what is cut away.** Pattern: the shape left after cutting. Jali: the light let through the holes.
3. **Both protect privacy without hiding everything.** A jali lets you see out but not in; good tailoring reveals the shape but not the body.
4. **Both are repeated by hand from a master.** Patterns are traced again and again; jali panels repeat one motif across a wall.
5. **Both carry instructions in their geometry.** Notches tell the tailor what joins what; a jali's star grid tells the mason where every cut goes.

## Natural connections
| Property | Pattern | Jali | Shared truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colour | Pale paper, chalk blue/yellow marks, graphite pencil | White marble, red sandstone, deep shadow | A pale, matte ground with sharp dark lines |
| Shape / geometry | Straight grain, curves only where the body needs them | Square grid rotated into 8-point stars | Rigour first, curves earned |
| Material / texture | Paper, chalk dust, pencil | Polished stone, chisel marks | Matte, tactile, handled |
| Structure | Pieces joined at notches | Cells joined in a lattice | A system of modules that lock together |
| Movement | Flat → folded → assembled | Static stone, *moving* light and shadow | Stillness made alive by light and assembly |
| Pattern / repetition | Same piece, cut 2 | Same motif, repeated across the wall | Repetition with purpose |
| Light & shadow | Shows on the cut edge | It is a machine for shadow | Shadow is part of the design |
| Imperfection | Hand pencil marks, chalk smudge | Hand-chiselled, slightly uneven cells | Handmade precision |
| Symbolism | Making, craft, the tailor (darzi) | Heritage, dignity, Lahore | Craft rooted in place |

## Design language (selected discoveries)
| Discovery | Website application |
|---|---|
| **Cut-away defines form** | Images are revealed through **apertures**: pattern-piece and lattice-cell shaped masks, not rectangles. The site's signature framing device |
| **Shadow as material** | A slow, soft **lattice shadow** drifts across key surfaces (hero, collection rooms) as if light came through a jali. One orchestrated ambient moment, off when reduced motion is set |
| **Notation as instruction** | Product details use **pattern notation**: grainline arrows, notch marks, "CUT 2", fold lines. Fabric facts are written like a tailor's marks (*Cotton twill · 210 GSM · grain: warp*) |
| **Modules that lock together** | Layout grid from the jali module (a square cell with 45° diagonals). Collections are walls of cells of different sizes; the **Style Studio** assembles pieces at their notches |
| **Flat → assembled** | Motion grammar: things arrive *flat* and *assemble* (pieces slide to join at a notch) instead of fading up. Page transitions cut along a seam line |

## Central concept: **Cut Light**
A garment is a flat sheet cut until it can hold a body. A jali is a flat stone cut until it can hold light. **Cut Light** builds the store from the same act: precise cuts in a flat surface that open up space. The site starts flat and pale, like paper or marble. Every image, collection and product is *revealed through a cut*, and soft lattice light moves across it like late afternoon in a Lahore courtyard. The tailor's notation explains how each piece is made. The Style Studio lets the customer do the final construction themselves: style isn't put on, it's assembled.

## How it drives the experience
- **Hero:** a full-height composition of one garment revealed through a large pattern-piece aperture, with lattice shadow slowly crossing it. A single statement line. No "SHOP NOW" pill.
- **Typography:** one variable grotesk (Archivo) whose **width axis** is the brand's material: condensed and heavy for structure (headlines), normal for reading. Width shifts slightly on scroll, so the type itself is "constructed". A narrow mono is used *only* for pattern notation.
- **Colour:** Marble (ground), Graphite (ink), Chalk Blue (the tailor's mark: links, notches, focus, the one accent), Stone Shadow (lattice). Sandstone appears only inside photography environments, never as UI paint.
- **Layout:** jali-module grid, with asymmetric cells; generous negative space; images cropped hard to details.
- **Graphic elements & shapes:** the notch (a small triangle cut into an edge) is the signature mark: on buttons, section breaks, the cursor on desktop and price tags. Dashed cut-lines mark interactive edges.
- **Motion & transitions:** assemble, don't fade. Pieces slide to meet at notches; apertures open like a cut; shadows drift. Mostly 400–700 ms with a firm ease-out; nothing bounces.
- **Navigation:** a slim bar with the brand and bag; the full menu opens as a **pattern sheet**: categories laid out as labelled pattern pieces.
- **Backgrounds & texture:** matte marble with very faint paper grain; no gradients, no glass.
- **Interactions:** hover on a product cuts its aperture slightly wider (reveals more); fabric swatches tilt to catch the raking light; the Style Studio snaps pieces together at their notches.
- **Page-to-page continuity:** the aperture of a clicked product expands into the product page hero (a shared-element transition), so you go "through the cut".

## Brand name shortlist **[proposal]** (a setting a buyer can change)

|---|---|---|

## Alternatives considered
1. **"Frame & Skin": Pattern × Paper kite (Basant).** Light frames, a stretched skin, lift and tension. Rejected for now: festive and playful, fights "sophisticated".
2. **"Signatures": Garment × Bookbinding.** Collections as bound volumes, products as folded signatures, sewn spines. Strong editorial fit, but less rooted in place and less visual than light through stone.

## Innovation check
- *What has local menswear never seen online?* Fabric explained like a material study, and shadow used as an interface.
- *The moment visitors will describe:* "the shirt appears through a cut, with light moving over it like through a window screen".
- *Product as an interactive experience:* the Style Studio is built on the brand's own grammar (assemble at notches), not a bolted-on widget.
